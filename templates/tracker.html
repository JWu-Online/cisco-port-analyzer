Device tracker

Reads every switch on your list over SNMP (read-only) and writes down where each device is plugged in. Each scan is compared with the ones before, so you can see what moved, what's new and what went missing. See a made-up campus

SNMP login

A read-only SNMP login on the switches. Ask whoever manages them for one that lets this PC's address in (SNMPv3 if you can: it's encrypted).

Daily scan

Windows Task Scheduler runs it as you, while you're logged on (the PC can be locked). If the PC is off or you're logged out at that time, that day is skipped.

      How it decides what moved
      
      
        
        
      • Where a device is: the port where it said who it is (LLDP or CDP), otherwise the port where
          the switch learned its MAC address. Ports that lead to another switch or an access point are skipped (MACs
          there are only passing through), and so is a port with more than 40 MACs.
        • 
        
      • Moved: seen on a different switch or port than the last place it was seen.
          New: never seen before. VLAN changed: same port, different VLAN.
        • 
        
      • Missing: not seen for 3 scans in a row on a switch that was
          read fine (a switch that couldn't be read doesn't count against its devices). Back: seen again
          after it went missing.
        • 
        
      • A device is known by its MAC address (or its name, when it only sent a name). A computer that plugs in by
          Wi-Fi some days won't be on a switch port those days.
